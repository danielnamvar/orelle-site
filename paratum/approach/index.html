    Tax strategy

    Where others see walls, Paratum sees doors. Its tax work is for situations where standard planning has run out of answers: a large gain, a high-income year, a concentrated position, an inheritance.

    Mitigation and deferral

    Mitigation reduces the tax owed. Deferral moves it to a later year, when rates, income or circumstances may be more favorable, and keeps the capital working in the meantime. Most strategies do some of both. The right mix depends on the client's income, assets, state of residence and plans, which is why every structure starts from the client's facts rather than a product.

    When it matters most

    • The sale of a business, real estate or a concentrated stock position
    • A year of unusually high ordinary income
    • Recurring high income from a practice or operating business
    • A family with income spread unevenly across generations or states
    • A large portfolio growing in a fully taxable account

    Oil and gas

    A direct investment in oil and gas drilling is one of the few places the tax code still allows most of an investment to be deducted in the year it is made.

    What it is

    The investor takes a working interest in one or more wells through a partnership or program run by an operator. Most of the cost of drilling, the labor, fuel, chemicals and site preparation known as intangible drilling costs (IDCs), is generally deductible in the year incurred. Equipment costs are depreciated over several years. Once a well produces, a portion of its income is sheltered by the depletion allowance.

    Who it fits

    Investors with high ordinary income in the current year, from a practice, a business or a liquidity event, who can hold an illiquid investment for several years and accept the risks of drilling. Because a working interest is not treated as a passive activity for this purpose, the deduction can offset active income, which is what makes it unusual.

    How it works

    1. Paratum reviews the client's income and the size of the deduction that makes sense.
    2. It identifies operators and programs, and coordinates diligence on their track record, geology and terms.
    3. The client's CPA and tax counsel confirm the expected treatment before any commitment.
    4. The client invests directly with the operator; the deduction flows through on the program's tax reporting.

    Working and royalty interests

    A working interest carries the costs, the deductions and the operational risk. A royalty interest receives a share of production without bearing costs, and offers depletion but not the drilling deduction. Many investors hold both over time.

    Risks and considerations

    Wells can be dry or produce less than expected. Commodity prices move. The investment is illiquid, and a working interest can carry liability that is usually addressed through the program's structure. Deductions can be recaptured on sale, and the alternative minimum tax and state rules can change the result. The quality of the operator matters more than the tax benefit.

    Who Paratum brings in

    Energy tax counsel, the client's CPA, independent petroleum engineers for reserve reviews, and established operators.

    Questions

    How much of the investment is deductible in the first year?

    It depends on the program's mix of intangible and tangible costs and the timing of drilling. Many programs target a majority of the investment as deductible in the first year; the client's tax counsel confirms the figure for a specific program.

    Is this the same as an oil and gas fund?

    Not necessarily. Many funds hold royalty or non-operated interests that do not pass through the drilling deduction. The structure determines the tax result.

    PPLI

    Private placement life insurance places an investment portfolio inside a life insurance policy, where it can grow without current income tax and pass to heirs income-tax-free.

    What it is

    PPLI is a variable universal life policy offered privately to qualified buyers. The premium is invested in insurance-dedicated funds or separately managed accounts, which can include hedge fund, private credit and other strategies that are normally tax-inefficient. Insurance costs are typically lower than in retail policies because the policy is designed for investment rather than for the death benefit.

    Who it suits

    Individuals and families with substantial investable assets, a long horizon and a portfolio that generates heavy annual tax, particularly from income-producing or actively traded strategies. It is often combined with a trust so the policy sits outside the taxable estate as well.

    How it works

    1. Paratum assesses whether the client's assets, horizon and goals justify a policy.
    2. It coordinates the carrier, a licensed agent, the investment managers and the trust structure.
    3. Premiums are paid in, usually over several years, and invested inside the policy.
    4. Growth inside the policy is not currently taxed; the death benefit passes to beneficiaries generally free of income tax, and policy loans can provide access during life.

    The rules that matter

    The policy must meet the tax code's definition of life insurance and its diversification requirements, and the policyholder may not direct the specific investments, a limit known as the investor control doctrine. A policy funded too quickly can become a modified endowment contract, which changes how withdrawals and loans are taxed. These rules are why design and ongoing administration matter.

    Who Paratum brings in

    Insurance carriers that offer private placement policies, licensed agents, insurance-dedicated fund managers, trust counsel and the client's CPA. Policies are placed only by licensed professionals.

    Paratum is not an insurance agency and does not sell insurance. See Disclosures.

    Income shifting

    Income shifting moves income from the person who would pay the highest rate to a family member, trust or entity that pays less, within rules that require the shift to be real.

    The main approaches

    Family limited partnerships and LLCs

    A family partnership holds investments or a business. Interests are transferred to children or trusts, and the income follows the ownership. Transfers can also qualify for valuation discounts for gift and estate tax purposes.

    Non-grantor trusts

    A trust that is taxed as its own taxpayer can, when properly designed and located, reduce or avoid state income tax on investment income, and distributions to beneficiaries in lower brackets carry the income with them.

    Entity structures

    The choice of entity, and how owners and family members who genuinely work in a business are compensated, changes how and where income is taxed. Management companies and holding structures can also separate income streams that are taxed differently.

    Who it fits

    Families with high income concentrated in one generation, owners of closely held businesses, and residents of high-tax states with investment income that need not be earned there.

    Risks and considerations

    The tax law ignores shifts that lack substance: income from services is taxed to the person who performs them, children's investment income can be taxed at their parents' rates, and states pursue trusts they consider resident. Control, ownership and economics must actually change. Paratum's work is to design a structure that will hold up, with counsel who will defend it.

    Who Paratum brings in

    Tax and trust counsel, the client's CPA, independent trustees and valuation firms.

    Not tax advice. See Disclosures.

    Real estate

    Real estate remains the most flexible tax asset most families own. The deductions can be accelerated, the gain can be deferred, and with planning it can pass to heirs at a stepped-up basis.

    Cost segregation and bonus depreciation

    A cost segregation study separates a building's components, such as fixtures, finishes and site work, from the structure itself. Those components are depreciated over five to fifteen years rather than twenty-seven and a half or thirty-nine, and may qualify for bonus depreciation in the first year. The result is a large early deduction that can offset other income where the owner qualifies.

    1031 exchanges

    Selling investment property and reinvesting the proceeds in like-kind property within the statutory deadlines defers the gain. Exchanges can be repeated over a lifetime, and the deferred gain may never be taxed if the property passes at death.

    Delaware statutory trusts

    A DST lets an owner exchange into a fractional interest in institutional property, such as a portfolio of apartments or industrial buildings, without managing it. It suits owners who want to keep deferring but stop operating.

    Opportunity zone funds

    Capital gains from any source can be reinvested in a qualified opportunity fund, deferring the original gain, and appreciation on the new investment held long enough can be excluded from tax.

    Who Paratum brings in

    Cost segregation engineers, qualified intermediaries for exchanges, DST and fund sponsors, real estate tax counsel and lenders.

    Not tax advice or an offer of any security. See Disclosures.

    M&A advisory

    In most transactions tax is settled at the end. Paratum starts with it, because the structure of a deal often moves after-tax proceeds more than the price does.

    Sell-side

    The most valuable work happens one to two years before a sale. Paratum helps owners decide between an asset sale and a stock sale, use installment terms and rollover equity (keeping a stake in the buyer to defer tax on part of the price), move shares into trusts before value is fixed, and qualify for the small business stock exclusion where it applies. It then coordinates the tax counsel and advisers who carry the transaction.

    Buy-side

    For acquirers, structure determines whether the purchase price becomes depreciable basis. Paratum works on structures that give the buyer a step-up in the basis of the assets acquired, and on how the price is allocated among them, so the deductions that follow are as large as the facts allow.

    Tax attribute monetization

    Tax attributes are tax assets a company carries: net operating losses (NOLs, past losses that can offset future income), capital loss carryforwards and tax credits. Many sit unused on the books of companies that will never earn enough to absorb them.

    Paratum identifies these attributes, values them, and finds a buyer, merger partner or structure that can use them. Section 382 caps how much of a company's NOLs can be used each year after a change in ownership, so the value of an attribute depends heavily on how the transaction is built. Paratum also advises on selling eligible clean-energy credits for cash under the transferability rules.

    Restructurings and recapitalizations

    A restructuring or bankruptcy can erase tax attributes or preserve them. Paratum works with restructuring counsel so that losses and credits survive a reorganization where possible, and become part of the value recovered.

    Who Paratum brings in

    M&A and tax counsel, accountants for quality-of-earnings and tax due diligence, valuation firms, lenders, and registered investment banks where a transaction requires one.

    Questions

    Can a company's NOLs be sold on their own?

    Not directly. Losses belong to the company, so they are realized through a merger, acquisition or restructuring that lets a profitable business use them, within the Section 382 limits.

    When should a business owner start planning a sale?

    Ideally one to two years ahead. Many of the most effective steps, such as gifts to trusts or changes in entity, must be taken before a buyer is in view.

    Asset protection

    Protection is strongest when it is built before it is needed. A structure in place years before a claim is hard to reach; the same structure put in place after a claim can be undone.

    Why timing matters

    Transfers made to avoid a creditor who already exists, or one who is reasonably foreseeable, can be reversed by a court under fraudulent transfer law. Planning done in calm years, for legitimate reasons such as estate planning and business succession, is far more durable. Paratum's protection work is therefore mostly preventive, and part of a family's broader plan rather than a reaction to a lawsuit.

    Protection structures

    No single structure protects everything. A durable plan layers several, so that a claimant has to get through each of them, and the cost of trying exceeds what is likely to be recovered.

    Domestic asset protection trusts

    Several states, among them Nevada, South Dakota and Delaware, allow a person to create an irrevocable trust of which they remain a discretionary beneficiary, with assets protected from future creditors after a waiting period. Nevada's two-year period is among the shortest. A trust of this kind works best for a resident of, or assets located in, a state that recognizes it.

    Offshore trusts

    Trusts in jurisdictions such as the Cook Islands and Nevis do not recognize foreign judgments, impose short limitation periods and high burdens of proof on creditors, and require a creditor to sue locally. They offer the strongest protection available, at higher cost and with strict reporting obligations to the IRS. Assets can remain invested with U.S. institutions until a threat appears.

    Layered entities

    Limited liability companies formed in states with strong charging order protection, such as Wyoming, Nevada and Delaware, separate risky assets from safe ones and limit a creditor of an owner to distributions the company chooses to make. Holding each property or business in its own entity keeps a claim against one from reaching the others.

    Equity stripping

    Equity stripping places legitimate, documented debt against an asset, typically real estate or a business, so that little equity remains exposed. The borrowed funds are invested elsewhere, often in protected structures. It must involve real lenders or real obligations on commercial terms; arrangements that exist only on paper can be disregarded.

    Privacy

    Ownership structures that keep a family's name off public records do not by themselves protect assets, but they reduce the chance of being targeted in the first place.

    Trusts and fiduciary

    A trust is only as good as the people who run it. Paratum helps families choose trustees, design how authority is divided, and oversee the trust for as long as it lasts.

    Choosing a trustee

    Family members know the family but may lack independence or expertise; banks offer permanence but often limited flexibility; independent trust companies sit between the two. Paratum helps match the trustee to the trust's purpose, and coordinates successor trustees so the trust never stalls.

    Directed trusts

    In a directed trust, the duties a single trustee would normally hold are divided. An investment adviser directs the portfolio, a distribution adviser decides payments, and an administrative trustee keeps the records. Families keep a trusted adviser in charge of investments while gaining a professional trustee in a favorable state.

    Trust situs

    Where a trust is based determines the law that governs it, its state income tax, how long it may last and how well it protects its assets. Nevada, South Dakota and Delaware lead on all four. An existing trust can often be moved to a better situs through decanting or a change of trustee.

    Private trust companies

    For larger families, a private trust company owned by the family can act as trustee for all of its trusts, keeping decisions in family hands under a formal governance structure.

    Ongoing oversight

    Paratum reviews trustee performance, fees, investment results and compliance with the trust's terms, and coordinates changes when the family's circumstances or the law move.

    Paratum is not a trustee or trust company. See Disclosures.

    Succession

    Succession planning decides whether wealth built in one generation reaches the next intact, and whether the next generation is ready to hold it.

    Dynasty trusts

    A dynasty trust holds assets for children, grandchildren and later generations, outside each of their estates. In states that allow trusts to last for centuries, assets can compound across generations without estate tax at each death, and with protection from beneficiaries' creditors and divorces.

    Estate freezes

    An estate freeze fixes the value of an asset in the senior generation's estate and passes future growth to heirs. Grantor retained annuity trusts (GRATs) and sales to intentionally defective grantor trusts are the common tools; both are most effective with assets expected to appreciate, such as a business before a sale.

    Family partnerships

    Holding assets in a family limited partnership or LLC allows interests to be transferred gradually while control stays with the senior generation, and transferred interests can qualify for valuation discounts.

    Business succession

    Passing an operating business means deciding who will own it, who will run it, and how those who do not take part are treated fairly. Paratum coordinates the legal, tax and valuation work and the agreements that make the transition stable.

    Family governance

    Family constitutions, councils and education for heirs give a family a way to make decisions together and prepare the next generation for responsibility, which often matters more than any document.

    Pre-litigation planning

    Most lawsuits are decided, in practice, before they are filed, when a plaintiff's attorney looks at what a defendant owns and how easily it can be reached.

    Who needs it

    Physicians and other professionals with malpractice exposure; executives and directors; owners of operating businesses with employees, vehicles or premises; real estate investors; and anyone whose wealth makes them a target out of proportion to their risk.

    What it involves

    1. An exposure review: which assets are reachable, through which claims, and in which states.
    2. Separation of risky assets from safe ones, and of each business or property into its own entity.
    3. Use of the protections the law already provides, such as retirement accounts, homestead and insurance.
    4. Trusts and other structures for what remains exposed, put in place while no claim exists.

    The legal limits

    Moving assets after a claim arises, or when one is foreseeable, can be reversed as a fraudulent transfer and can expose the people involved to liability. Paratum's work is preventive and is done with counsel who will stand behind it. If a dispute is already underway, the right first call is to litigation counsel.

    Not legal advice. See Disclosures.
