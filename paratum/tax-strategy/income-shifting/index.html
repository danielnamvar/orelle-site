    Income shifting

    Income shifting moves income from the person who would pay the highest rate to a family member, trust or entity that pays less, within rules that require the shift to be real.

    Key applications

    • Family limited partnerships and LLCs
    • Non-grantor trusts
    • Business entity structuring
    • Intergenerational ownership transfers
    • State income tax planning

    Who may benefit

    Families with high income concentrated in one generation, owners of closely held businesses, and residents of high-tax states with investment income that need not be earned there.

    Our role

    Paratum designs the strategy and coordinates the specialists who carry it out: tax and trust counsel, the client's CPA, independent trustees and valuation firms.

    Technical considerations

    Principal approaches

    Family limited partnerships and LLCs

    A family partnership holds investments or a business. Interests are transferred to children or trusts, and the income follows the ownership. Transfers can also qualify for valuation discounts for gift and estate tax purposes.

    Non-grantor trusts

    A trust that is taxed as its own taxpayer can, when properly designed and located, reduce or avoid state income tax on investment income, and distributions to beneficiaries in lower brackets carry the income with them.

    Entity structures

    The choice of entity, and how owners and family members who genuinely work in a business are compensated, changes how and where income is taxed. Management companies and holding structures can also separate income streams that are taxed differently.

    Risks and considerations

    The tax law ignores shifts that lack substance: income from services is taxed to the person who performs them, children's investment income can be taxed at their parents' rates, and states pursue trusts they consider resident. Control, ownership and economics must actually change. Paratum's work is to design a structure that will hold up, with counsel who will defend it.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is income shifting?

    Legitimately moving income-producing assets or business interests to family members, trusts or entities that are taxed at lower rates or in lower-tax states.

    Can a trust reduce state income tax?

    A properly structured non-grantor trust may avoid state income tax on investment income, depending on where the trust is administered, who its trustees and beneficiaries are, and where the income is sourced.

    Can income shifting be combined with estate planning?

    Yes. Transferring ownership interests can serve both purposes, moving income today and future appreciation out of the estate.

    What limits apply?

    The assignment-of-income doctrine, economic substance requirements, gift tax, the kiddie tax and state residency rules for trusts all have to be addressed.
